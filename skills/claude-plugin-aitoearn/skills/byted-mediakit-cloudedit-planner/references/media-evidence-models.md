# Evidence for Editorial Decisions

Start from an unresolved decision, not a media genre or a list of everything a tool can describe. Use only the dimensions that can change the edit. Existing source-linked understanding, verified transcripts, and exact user instructions may already answer the question.

| Decision | Evidence to establish |
| --- | --- |
| What is worth retaining? | Relevant visible/audible content, complete meaning or action, usable source boundaries, contribution to the user's purpose |
| What context is necessary? | Referents, prerequisites, questions and responses, state changes and outcomes, with observed facts distinguished from interpretation |
| How do pieces relate? | Supported identity, time/place or causal relationships, complementary views, contrasts, repetitions; mark uncertain connections |
| Where can a cut work? | Speech/action completeness, entering and leaving visual states, direction and position, continuing sound, missing context at the boundary |
| What should lead the pacing? | Time needed to comprehend speech, inspect details, read visible text, perceive an action or sustain a requested feeling; relevant sound phrases/events |
| How should sound be used? | Actual speech, music, ambience or sound events, source-time boundaries, overlap, intelligibility and correspondence to picture |
| Can a composition work? | Visible subjects, important details/text, spatial bounds and regions that must remain visible; actual content of standalone images |
| Is processing necessary? | An observed defect or compatibility limit, where it occurs, what required content it obscures, and whether selection or editable adjustment already solves it |

These are questions to select from, not mandatory sections of every analysis result. Do not request detail merely to fill a record. Do not invent numeric confidence scores or exact timing from coarse evidence.

## Match Evidence to the Source

Videos may supply motion and sound, but understanding their frames does not establish the speech or music. Images can supply subjects, composition, text, and visible states; they do not establish motion, before/after events, or sound. Separate audio can carry meaning and timing independently of video, but does not prove what was visible. Inspect each source needed for a content-dependent use.

Metadata establishes technical properties, not meaning. Filenames may suggest questions, but do not establish identity, chronology, mood, claims, or quality. Similar people or objects do not by themselves prove sources show the same event or are interchangeable takes.

For dialogue-dependent decisions, use available ASR under [Editorial Evidence Requirements](media-understanding.md), even without subtitles. Combine it with visual or sound evidence when needed for the decision; verified speech alone does not require another model call. Reuse sufficient ASR with proven provenance and coverage; preserve complete utterances, source timing, and uncertain words. A summary or OCR result is not verified dialogue. An empty transcript does not prove silence or describe non-speech sound.

When exact synchronization or boundaries matter, obtain timing at the required precision. Preserve original time offsets and any processing mappings. Sampled frames may miss brief actions; broad sound descriptions do not establish precise beats. Ask for focused inspection when that uncertainty changes the edit.

## Close a Gap

Specify the affected source/range, the blocked decision, and the visual or audible fact needed. Retrieve the result, check whether it answers that question, and update the decision. Continue independent decisions while results are pending. If a fact cannot be established, retain the uncertainty or choose an adequately supported alternative within the user's constraints.

Stop gathering evidence when it is sufficient for the intended decisions and requested analysis coverage. More description is not inherently a better basis for editing.
